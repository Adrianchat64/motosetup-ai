How to read the ratios and comparison

Final drive ratio = rear sprocket teeth ÷ front sprocket teeth. A higher ratio is shorter gearing: more torque multiplication at the rear wheel and more engine RPM at the same road speed. A lower ratio is taller gearing: less torque multiplication and less RPM at the same road speed.

At the same engine RPM, theoretical speed changes by the inverse ratio. Exact absolute speeds need primary reduction, the selected transmission gear ratio, and measured rear-tire rolling circumference. The percentage comparisons need only the sprocket counts, assuming all other gearing stays the same.

Track-performance interpretation

Calculated gearing changes are mathematical comparisons. Real track performance depends on engine powerband, traction, terrain, rider, and shift points.

Shorter gearing can sharpen low-speed response and increase rear-wheel torque multiplication, but may require more shifts and reach the rev limit sooner. Taller gearing can reduce shifting and lower RPM at speed, but may feel less responsive in slow sections. These are tradeoffs, not lap-time or top-speed promises.
